Omni’s tools have always struck me as complex, powerful, and guided by their own philosophy. OmniFocus™ is no exception.

I used it to manage university courses, self-study, life plans, and IELTS preparation. Tasks arranged in an outline made large goals easier to break into clear levels and next actions.

If you have read Getting Things Done and want a mature tool for practicing GTD, you will probably consider OmniFocus. If configuration and plain-text workflows appeal to you, you might also explore [Org mode](/blog/void-calendar-vs-org-mode).

## A powerful system also needs maintenance

OmniFocus helped me organize complex work, but I also spent time maintaining projects, tags, and task states. Back then, I mostly managed that myself. I wanted AI to help organize thoughts, break down tasks, and arrange time.

OmniFocus supports pasted images, attachments, and rich-text notes. For me, that still did not provide the full document-editing experience I wanted from tools like Word, Notion, or Typora. See [OmniFocus notes and attachments](https://support.omnigroup.com/documentation/omnifocus/universal/4.9.3/en/inspector/).

The pace of updates and waiting for synchronization affected my experience too. The bigger blockage, though, was the growing Inbox.

## I could capture more than I could organize

I captured hundreds of items but did not have time to organize them.

Many were only thoughts: something I might study, a project I might start, or something I hoped to do at a distant stage of life. They sounded appealing, but were far from work I had decided to begin.

Opening OmniFocus meant seeing those unprocessed items again. They were not commitments yet, but occupied my task system until I increasingly avoided dealing with the Inbox.

That experience led to Idea in Void Calendar.

An Idea is the early form of a task, before you have decided whether to do it. Capture a passing thought there. When you decide to act, turn it into a task, break it into steps, and arrange time.

Capturing a thought does not have to become a promise to your future self.

## Clear the Idea list and keep the record

Ideas accumulate too. Some remain worth pursuing, while others were simply attractive at the time.

Tell Void AI which part to organize. For example, ask it to remove Ideas related to an old plan you no longer intend to pursue. It can process that selection and make the list clearer again.

Each Idea has a corresponding note with the same name, where its content is stored. The Idea navigation displays those entries. Removing an entry from that view keeps its note, so you can still find the content later.

By default, the note names are recorded in the daily note for the day AI removes the entries. Click a name to return to the original content. Set aside thoughts you are not pursuing while retaining a record of what you considered.

## More capabilities should still feel lighter

Void Calendar also supports Dynamic Island, so you can see what you are working on and quickly capture new thoughts. Record an idea, then return to the current task.

I want Void Calendar to support more of the process: Ideas, tasks, notes, calendars, and AI-assisted organization and execution. Everyday interaction should stay light as those capabilities grow.

I am the designer of Void Calendar. I have ADHD and lean toward the P, or Perceiving, side of personality types. I will keep improving the software to make it simpler and reduce the friction of capturing something or starting work.

If something feels awkward, join the user community and tell me. I will promptly consider how to make it easier to use.
